Fixes #327. Supersedes #358. Based on #407, which even-odd rings need; the commits after it are this PR's.

The fringe that antialiases a fill's edges gives the fill about a pixel of ink across however thin it is: a sliver a tenth of a pixel wide comes out nearly eight times as dark as its area, a 0.3 px hairline nearly three times. A fill whose mean width (twice its area over its perimeter, a hole's area taken off under the fill's rule) is under a pixel, whose bounds span at most 65,536 pixels and whose operation coverage bounds is now rasterized on the CPU by the clip masks' exact-area coverage into one 2048 x 2048 one-byte atlas, and drawn as a quad through `GlyphTexture::AlphaMask`: paint, scissor and clips apply as they do to text, on both backends, with no shader change. A mask is found again by request (fill rule, path, transform, tolerances and target size, bit for bit) or by outline (to 1/256 px from its corner); a frame uploads the rectangle its new masks span; at most 256 are made a frame, as Graphite's `RasterPathAtlas`, past which a thin fill keeps its fringe; the atlas starts over when it fills or when a frame finds none of the masks the frame before made. `Canvas::set_thin_fill_masks(false)` turns it off.

The further frames are where an exact thin fill meets a draw beside or over it, each composited by its own coverage (fox-with-box at 0.5x 697 -> 899; software Chromium 908, Firefox 916), and one icon whose light sliver had half hidden the over-inked thin tip of the thick path beneath it: a whole path's mean width does not see a thin part of a thick path.

**Cost.** One binary with the masks on and off, the 365 files whose frames change, 1x and 1080p: still -1.1 % in sum, median frame +0.1 %; every frame moved by 0.37 px, so that each mask is made again each frame, -0.2 % in sum, median +0.1 %. Files whose frames do not change +0.1 %, median 0.0 %; card pages +0.0 to +0.2 % instructions.

A moving scene makes its masks again each frame, so the second commit has the rasterizer, which clip masks share, resolve row by row from the edges crossing each row, where it had sorted every cell an edge touched. On the efficiency cores of an M4 Max, rasterizing the masks of moving corpus frames takes 54 % fewer cycles and clip masks 32 % fewer, with about 80 % fewer L1D misses and 63 % fewer branch mispredicts; 10 of 11,403 frames move, by one level. On an Apple TV 4K (A15) diners at 1080p in motion, the costliest frame, takes +36 % render-thread cycles over masks off on the efficiency cores, +64 % before. Its GPU does no more work with the masks (GPU time -0.6 %, bandwidth within 1 %, no partial renders either way).

The third commit has the atlas start at 512 pixels a side and grow in the frame a mask finds no room, where it took 2048 x 2048 bytes and its CPU copy as much again for the first thin fill: on the M4 the masks of diners, the tiger or puzzle-fox at 1080p add half a MiB of peak footprint where they added 11, and a frame that needs the whole atlas, as one tall sliver of an icon at 1080p does, still adds 11. Every corpus frame renders as with the atlas at its largest. Still a draft: the rest of the cost in motion comes next.
