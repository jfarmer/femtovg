The analytic tier of #346, and what a clip or a scissor does to a draw that shares an edge with it. A `clip_path` whose path outlines a rectangle (under any transform), a rounded rectangle or an ellipse is fitted to a box with elliptical corners and carried by each draw under it in two more uniform rows; the fragment shader turns it into the pixel's share inside the box. The clip's edge is antialiased, and the clip costs no stencil draws, no replay and no pass. Only a draw under a shape runs the shader variant that evaluates one, scissored to the pixels the shape reaches. A rectangle that cuts a box with round corners - a window's content under the window - and a rectangle rounded at the two corners of one side - its title bar - clip in the scissor's place: the rounded box stays in force, and each draw carries the rectangle, joined with the scissor, along the sides of it that cut into the box. Other paths clip on the stencil as before, as do a shape that partly overlaps the one in force in any other way, a box thinner than a pixel, and a shape drawn under with a composite operation coverage cannot bound (`Copy`, `SourceIn`, `SourceOut`, `DestinationIn`, `DestinationAtop`).

An edge a draw has in common with the clip is antialiased once where the two can be told to share it: a draw that stays inside the clip is not clipped - by its bounds, or by the points of its outline, as the clip's own path filled under it is -, an upright rect under an upright rect clip or cut is cut to it, one that covers a rounded clip is drawn as the clip, and nested clips that all but coincide count once. Chromium's GPU rasterizer does the same, but for a turned clip filled with its own outline, and a rounded one at some scales, which it and the other browsers cover twice. The scissor is a box like the clip and meets a draw by the same rules, so a draw that ends on the scissor's edge - the viewport of an export - no longer takes the scissor's coverage over its own. Any other clip's coverage multiplies the draw's, as in every browser.

### Cost

1080p on an M4 Max, master -> PR, medians of paired runs ([pages and scripts](https://github.com/rebeckerspecialties/femtovg/tree/demo-assets/harness/clip-bench)); OpenGL as measured at 620b19c, whose fragment shaders the later commits leave as they are:

| scene | wgpu | OpenGL |
|---|---:|---:|
| 200 cards, each under a rounded-rect clip | 12.5 -> 2.85 ms | 6.9 -> 0.64 ms |
| 200 cards, no clip | 2.37 -> 2.42 ms | 0.40 -> 0.41 ms |
| 40 full-screen fills, no clip | 1.27 -> 1.26 ms | 0.77 -> 0.77 ms |
| the same under a rect clip | 1.36 -> 1.25 ms | 0.78 -> 0.76 ms |
| under a rounded-rect clip | 1.36 -> 1.29 ms | 0.81 -> 0.96 ms |
| under an ellipse clip | 1.33 -> 1.33 ms | 0.79 -> 1.13 ms |
| under a rounded-rect clip of 400 x 300 | 1.34 -> 0.77 ms | 0.37 -> 0.18 ms |
| 40 full-screen paths under that clip | 1.32 -> 0.81 ms | 0.48 -> 0.17 ms |

The cost moves from the clip to the fragments under it. A draw under a shape is scissored to what the shape reaches, so a clip smaller than its content costs less than its stencil did; rects under a rect clip are cut to it; a draw the clip holds whole carries none unless the draw before it did (switching shader variants for it cost the clipped cards 28 % on OpenGL). Fill-bound content that crosses a clip as large as the target pays per fragment: nothing on wgpu, 19 % under a rounded rect and 45 % under an ellipse on Apple's OpenGL. Recording a fill costs the canvas 5 to 10 ns more than master's 230 to 310 - the 600 unclipped fills of the cards page take 0.8 % more instructions to record and encode - and a rect that a scissor, a rect clip or a cut cuts is flattened a second time, 0.3 us. A cut costs nothing per fragment: every draw evaluates its scissor box already. Over the corpus - every file at the fit and at 1080p, 2,392 frames - the frames take 5,933 -> 5,895 ms in sum and the median frame as long as it did (+0.0 %).
