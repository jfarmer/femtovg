# Cost of femtovg/femtovg#389 against master (2026-10-12)

master d70ffeb (`m389g`) against #389 at 86cb3de (`p389g`), both built with `--cfg harness_text`. `_notext` is the
same binary with `NO_TEXT=1`. Apple M4 Max, macOS 26.6.2, wgpu on Metal. Other work was running on the machine (load
average between 2.7 and 4.8 for the timing, 12 to 15 for the memory, soak and moving-scene runs), so every run is one
process at a time with the two builds alternating. The raw output, and the md5 of each binary, are in
`pr389-timing-2026-10-12.txt`.

#389 changes two things that run in every frame. Every fill and stroke takes one more argument, a transform for the
path, which is `None` unless the path is a glyph's outline. A glyph drawn as an outline has its transform passed
that way, where master set it on the canvas with a save, a translate, a scale and a restore.

Neither shows. Instructions per frame are the same on the two builds to within what repeated runs of one build
differ by, and frame times move by the same small amounts in both directions. Peak memory, render passes and heap
allocations per frame are the same too.

## OpenGL

The same two checkouts with femtovg's OpenGL renderer, on Apple's OpenGL (`build.sh --gl`). `gl_clip_check` passes on
both. `gl_svg_dump` draws a file's fills, strokes and text, with no clips, masks, filters or group opacities.

`clip-bench/gl_dump_ab.py m389 p389 1,3` over the 1,279 corpus files: of the 2,558 dumps 2,547 are bit for bit the
same on the two builds. The 11 that differ are of the eight text probes with a gradient: all eight at scale 1, and
at scale 3, where only the upper left third of a file is in the dump, the three that have text there. On those, #389's dump is
within 0.03 % of pixels of its own with the text drawn as paths (`TEXT_AS_PATHS=1`), and master's is 0.40 % to
33.19 % away. `pr389-gl-text.png` has three of them.

The twelve probes drawn 1:1 (scale 200/1024): ten are the same on the two builds. The two that differ are
`gradient-size-threshold` and `gradient-transformed-groups`, the two whose text is drawn as outlines at scale 1, as
on wgpu.

## Render passes and heap allocations

Render passes, from builds with `pass_count.py` applied (`--cfg harness_pass_count`): 777 on each build over the 134
frames with text and 15,630 on each over the 501 without, and no frame with more or fewer.

Heap allocations while a frame is recorded and encoded, from builds with `--cfg harness_alloc_count`, six frames a
run: with text on the allocator is called the same number of times on every one of the 134 frames (193,872 calls
when one frame of each is added up). With text off it is called the same number of times on 500 of the 501, and
once more on the other
(`babylon/sliders`, which gives 9,414 or 9,415 on either build from run to run). The bytes asked for are within
0.001 % in both sets.

## What this does not show

* A frame's time on a small GPU. This is a desktop one, and the frames are 1 to 35 ms. The instruction counts and the
  recording time are the processor's share, which is what #389 changes.
* The Raspberry Pi Zero model of `harness/pz/`. Its inputs were measured with an instrumented harness that is not
  on this branch. Of those inputs, the render passes, allocations, transient bytes and recording time are measured
  here and are the same on the two builds.
* The OpenGL ES 2.0 paths of the OpenGL backend: Apple's OpenGL is the desktop one.
* `fill_text`'s own layout. The harness gives femtovg glyphs that usvg has placed.
